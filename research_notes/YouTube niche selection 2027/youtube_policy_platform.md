## 1. YouTube Partner Program (YPP) changes: inauthentic content, reused content, AI disclosure, likeness detection, 2026 updates, and the February 2027 YPP overhaul

### Takeaway
YouTube does not ban AI. It demonetizes "inauthentic" content, which since July 2026 means three named types: (1) mass-produced, templated or repetitive videos; (2) "off-putting" emotionally manipulative clickbait such as staged animal rescues; (3) AI-generated personas giving health, finance, legal or medical advice. Separately, a YPP overhaul announced in August 2026 and taking effect 1 February 2027 doubles the entry bar for new channels (8,000 watch hours or 20M Shorts views). It also adds a floor that every partner must meet to keep earning from Shorts: 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days. The documented takedown waves, from fake AI trailers and fake AI true crime to the January 2026 removals of channels on Kapwing's list, hit channels that were deceptive, templated or spam-like, not channels that simply used AI.

### Cited Findings

**July 2025: "repetitious" renamed "inauthentic"**
- YouTube updated its YPP monetization policies effective 15 July 2025. It renamed the "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content" to make clear that it covers content that is mass-produced or repetitive — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-targets-mass-produced-content-in-monetization-update/550337/)
- Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie called it "a minor update to the company's long-standing YPP policies". He said the main change was the rename, so that "repetitious" content became "inauthentic" content and the policy explicitly includes mass-produced or repetitive content — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/youtube-responds-to-ai-concerns/)
- YouTube said its reused-content policy was unchanged. Commentary, compilations and reaction videos stay monetizable if they add significant original commentary, modification, or educational or entertainment value. The 2025 change was described as targeting spam, not reaction channels — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)
- At the time, coverage noted the change "doesn't specifically relate to AI-generated content". Early reporting also said YouTube had not defined thresholds for "mass produced" or "repetitious" — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-targets-mass-produced-content-in-monetization-update/550337/)

**July 2026: inauthentic content clarified into three named types (effective 16 July 2026)**
- YouTube updated its monetization policies to define which AI-generated and low-quality videos cannot earn. It named three types under "inauthentic content" — [TechCrunch, 20 Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
  1. Generic or templated, mass-produced content with little variation, including tutorials that copy existing material on the platform — [The AI Insider](https://theaiinsider.tech/2026/07/21/youtube-clarifies-monetization-policies-to-further-curb-ai-generated-slop-content/)
  2. "Off-putting" or distressing content made mainly to manipulate emotions for clicks, even when no AI was used. Staged animal distress or rescue videos are the stated example — [The AI Insider](https://theaiinsider.tech/2026/07/21/youtube-clarifies-monetization-policies-to-further-curb-ai-generated-slop-content/); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/youtube-cracks-down-on-off-putting-content-and-ai-slop-2000787956)
  3. AI-generated personas discussing sensitive subjects such as health care, finance, legal issues and medical advice — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-youtube-ai-video-monetization-rules/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2226445/youtube-explains-ai-slop-policy-why-some-creators-wont-get-paid/)
- Matt Halprin, YouTube's trust and safety head, said viewers find this content off-putting. He said channels with "excessive amounts" of any of the three types lose monetization eligibility, meaning the whole channel and not just the video — [Moneywise](https://moneywise.com/news/top-stories/youtube-ai-slop-inauthentic-content-monetization-rules); [The AI Insider](https://theaiinsider.tech/2026/07/21/youtube-clarifies-monetization-policies-to-further-curb-ai-generated-slop-content/)
- Coverage describes the update as adding specificity to existing guidelines, not as a new rule or a blanket AI ban — [Moneywise](https://moneywise.com/news/top-stories/youtube-ai-slop-inauthentic-content-monetization-rules); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2226445/youtube-explains-ai-slop-policy-why-some-creators-wont-get-paid/)

**AI disclosure: "altered or synthetic content" (introduced March 2024 (older); automatic labeling added May 2026)**
- Creators must disclose realistic content that a viewer could easily mistake for a real person, place, scene or event, when it was made with altered or synthetic media, including generative AI — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/)
- Disclosure is not required for clearly unrealistic or animated content, special effects, beauty or lighting filters, or generative AI used for productivity such as scripts, content ideas or automatic captions — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/)
- Since May 2026, YouTube detects AI content and labels it automatically, even without creator disclosure. It uses internal signals plus C2PA metadata and SynthID watermarks. The label sits below the player on long-form videos and as an overlay on Shorts. Creators can dispute a label in Studio — [C2PA Viewer](https://c2paviewer.com/articles/youtube-ai-labels-c2pa)
- The label cannot be removed from videos made with YouTube's own AI tools (Veo, Dream Screen) or from videos whose C2PA metadata marks them as fully generative — [Mahoning Matters](https://www.mahoningmatters.com/living/article315909201.html)
- YouTube says the label does not affect recommendations or monetization eligibility — [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/youtube-ai-labels-auto-detection)
- One guide says sensitive topics (health, news, elections, finance) can get a more prominent on-player label. A secondary source claims consistent non-disclosure risks removal or YPP suspension, but I could not confirm that in YouTube's own text — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/ai-disclosure-rules/); [AIR glossary](https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-is-youtubes-ai-content-disclosure-policy)

**Likeness detection (face, voice coming)**
- October 2025: launched for YPP creators. March 2026: piloted with journalists, government officials and political candidates. April 2026: extended to the entertainment industry, where talent agencies can enrol celebrities. May 2026: opened to all creators aged 18 and over — [MediaPost, 19 May 2026](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/415170/youtube-rolls-out-likeness-detection-to-all-creato.html); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/05/18/youtube-will-help-you-find-videos-featuring-your-ai-clone/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtubes-ai-face-shield-now-covers-every-creator-18-and-over/)
- At Made on YouTube (September 2026), YouTube said speaking-voice detection would be combined with face detection later in 2026. It is not yet clear whether voice-only clones without a face will be flagged. YouTube says "detection does not guarantee removal" and that parody and satire are preserved. The tool is labeled experimental and is unavailable in some countries — [Medianama, Sep 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-youtube-likeness-detection-voices-clones/)

**February 2027 YPP overhaul (announced August 2026)**
- From 1 February 2027, new YPP applicants need 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days, or 20M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, up from 4,000 hours or 10M views. The 1,000-subscriber minimum is unchanged — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/); [Cined](https://www.cined.com/youtube-makes-monetization-harder-new-partner-program-rules-explained/); [Tech Times, 11 Aug 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323954/20260811/youtube-partner-program-doubles-entry-bar-adds-monthly-shorts-earnings-gate.htm)
- New for all partners, existing ones included: Shorts ad and Premium revenue sharing requires 10M qualified Shorts views over a rolling 90 days. Channels below that line stay in YPP and keep earning on long-form. Shorts revenue resumes once they cross it again — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-updates-partner-program-terms-shorts-payout-rules/585331/); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-partner-program-2027-changes/); [Reap](https://reap.video/blog/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027)
- Existing partners keep their status. They must accept the new terms by 31 January 2027 — [Socialday](https://socialday.live/features/youtube-raises-partner-program-entry-bar-to-8-000-watch-hours-from-february-2027)
- YouTube says it is adding other earning routes for channels under the 10M Shorts threshold: Shopping bonuses, brand-deal incentives and trend-based earnings boosts. The update also states Premium revenue shares of 30% for Premium and 60% for Premium Lite, distributed by member watch time and views. That figure is reported via secondary summary of the blog, so confirm it against the original — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)
- One outlet framed the change as "Long-Form Has the Easier Path" — [Switcher Studio](https://www.switcherstudio.com/whats-new/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027); see also [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)
- Unconfirmed: a single secondary source reports a 90-day grace window for channels that fall below thresholds. Whether early-access members can still unlock ads under the old thresholds after 1 February 2027 is also unclear — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-updates-partner-program-terms-shorts-payout-rules/585331/) (summary of the gap)

- Critics argue legitimate faceless creators and multi-channel operations get caught as collateral, because shared templates, synced upload schedules and shared infrastructure look like slop networks. They also say YouTube has not published a granular line between acceptable AI-assisted work and "slop" — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage); [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/artificial-intelligence/how-google-machine-terminated-130000-ai-slop-youtube-channels-six-months-903645)

### Inferences
- The enforcement target is a combination: templated output, scale (many near-identical uploads or many linked channels), deception (fake trailers, fake true crime, fake rescues) and AI personas in high-stakes advice. AI as a production tool is not the target. A channel run from a reels generator that outputs near-identical formats at volume sits squarely in category 1 risk, and multi-channel farms add category-independent "cluster" risk.
- For a channel that starts monetizing in 2027, the bar is now 8,000 hours or 20M Shorts views. The Shorts path is also gated by an ongoing 10M views per 90 days floor. Long-form watch time is structurally the more reliable path to YPP and to Shorts-independent income.
- Health, finance and legal niches are not banned. Fronting them with a synthetic AI host or persona is now explicitly non-monetizable. Faceless formats in these niches need a real, accountable human voice or expert, or at least no fake persona. The same pressure favours credibility, the "respect" criterion.
- Under automatic labeling, photorealistic synthetic footage will carry a visible "altered or synthetic" label even when undisclosed. YouTube says the label is neutral for reach, but audience trust and credibility effects are untested (my inference).

### Gaps
- I could not access YouTube Help Center text directly (blocked fetch). Exact policy wording, especially for the three July 2026 categories and the "excessive amounts" threshold, is paraphrased from news coverage.
- YouTube has published no official count of channels demonetized or terminated under the inauthentic-content policy. The 130,000 figure is unconfirmed for YouTube.
- No confirmed info on an appeal or re-review timeline for inauthentic-content demonetization.
- Several "April 2026 purge" claims (for example a crackdown on "regenerated scripts") appear only on vendor blogs and could not be verified — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/ai-faceless-channel-monetization-youtube-policy).

## 2. How YouTube treated AI "slop" in 2025-2026 (statements, terminations) and which AI-assisted channels stay monetized

### Takeaway
Neal Mohan's January 2026 letter made "managing AI slop" a named 2026 priority, while YouTube keeps pushing its own AI tools: over 1M channels used them daily in December 2025. The working line from YouTube and the Creator Liaison is that AI is fine as long as the result is original, varied and honestly disclosed. Recommendation-level penalties now add to monetization penalties: in October 2026, Shorts started favouring original clips over re-uploads. I found no reliable, named examples of heavily AI-produced channels confirmed as fully monetized. That is a gap; vendor blogs assert many exist.

### Cited Findings
- On 21 January 2026, Mohan said reducing AI slop and detecting deepfakes are 2026 priorities. YouTube would "reduce the spread of low-quality AI content" by building on its systems against spam, clickbait and repetitive low-quality content — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/21/youtube-chief-says-managing-ai-slop-is-a-priority-for-2026-.html); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-21/youtube-ceo-says-battling-ai-slop-a-top-priority-in-2026); [Technave](https://technave.com/gadget/YouTube-takes-down-top-AI-Slop-channels-with-millions-of-subscribers-45593.html)
- The same letter called AI a "boon to creatives who are ready to lean in" and said more than 1M channels used YouTube's AI creation tools daily in December 2025. It previewed letting creators make Shorts with their own AI likeness — [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/youtube-channels-using-ai-tools-reduce-slop-neal-mohan-letter-1236636548/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/21/youtube-chief-says-managing-ai-slop-is-a-priority-for-2026-.html)
- The 2026 letter's four priorities, per coverage: reinventing entertainment, protecting kids and teens, powering the creator economy, and "supercharging" AI. It also said dismissing creators' work as mere "UGC" is over — [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/social-media/articles/youtube-ceo-neal-mohan-big-140000253.html); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/21/youtube-chief-says-managing-ai-slop-is-a-priority-for-2026-.html)
- **Scale of the slop problem (third-party):** in Kapwing's test with a fresh account, 104 of the first 500 Shorts (21%) were AI-generated and 165 (33%) were "brainrot". Kapwing notes only Google can say whether that reflects the algorithm or the upload mix — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/one-in-five-youtube-shorts-shown-to-new-users-is-ai-generated-slop-study-finds/); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/110735-over-21-youtube-now-ai-slop-report.html)
- **Slop channel economics (Kapwing estimates from Social Blade):**
  - Bandar Apna Dost (India): about 2.07B views and about $4.25M per year.
  - Three Minutes Wisdom (South Korea): about $4M per year.
  - Top ten slop channels combined: about $33.6M per year.
  - By total slop views, South Korea leads with 8.45B, then Pakistan (5.34B) and the US (3.39B). Spain has the most combined slop subscribers (20.22M).
  — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/); [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/12/03/study-finds-ai-slop-videos-spreading-fast-across-youtube-and-theres-a-lot-of-money-being-made/)
- **Recommendation-level change (1 October 2026):** a TeamYouTube post and a Creator Insider video by Rene Ritchie said Shorts recommendations would favour original content and cut reach for re-uploads and aggregator or clipping channels. Per Ritchie, voice-over narration describing what is on screen, minor technical edits and template-based bulk changes do not make someone else's clip original; your own commentary, editing or storytelling does. No start date or re-evaluation timeline was given. The change affects recommendations only; monetization stays under the reused-content policy — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/youtube-shorts-promoting-original-content-reposts-3718668/); [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-cracks-down-on-clipping-channels-in-shorts-update-prioritizing-original-content-3415700/); [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774/)
- It is unclear whether outputs from YouTube's own AI remix tools are treated as "original" under this change — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/youtube-downranks-re-uploaded-shorts-ai-remix-status-unclear)
- TeamYouTube has reportedly confirmed that AI-enhanced videos remain eligible if properly disclosed. Disclosure alone reportedly does not limit reach or monetization — [Technave](https://technave.com/gadget/YouTube-takes-down-top-AI-Slop-channels-with-millions-of-subscribers-45593.html); [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/youtube-ai-labels-auto-detection)
- **What remains monetizable, per policy guidance:** AI used as a tool (script drafts, editing, translation, disclosed voiceovers) stays YPP-eligible. Reviewers look for evidence of human creative decisions: a consistent editorial stance, a defined audience, a recurring analytical framework, and each video materially different from the last. These are vendor-guide interpretations, not YouTube text — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/ai-faceless-channel-monetization-youtube-policy); [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/guides/youtube-ai-content-policy); [MilX](https://milx.app/en/trends/does-ai-impact-youtube-monetization-what-youtube-has-to-say)
- YouTube's showcased examples of the "new studios" in the 2026 letter were human-led scripted creators: Dhar Mann and Alan Chikin Chow, who have built their own studios — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/01/21/neal-mohans-2026-letter-to-creators-a-lot-of-ai-a-tiktok-shop-competitor-and-beautifully-produced-tv-to-compete-with-netflix/)
- **Kids and AI pressure:** a March 2026 coalition letter led by Fairplay asked YouTube to label AI content, bar it from YouTube Kids, and add a parental AI toggle that is off by default. This is an advocacy position, not YouTube policy — [Fairplay for Kids](https://fairplayforkids.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/YouTube-Letter-AI-Slop.pdf)
- A cautionary data point from a vendor blog: one creator spent $26,311 over 150 days on an AI faceless channel and lost nearly $10,000 — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/ai-faceless-channel-monetization-youtube-policy) (single anecdote, unverified)

### Inferences
- YouTube's posture is dual-track. It promotes AI tools such as Veo, Gemini editing and auto-dubbing, while demonetizing and downranking low-variation output. The safest faceless or AI-assisted positioning is "AI-accelerated production of an original, human-authored editorial product", for example researched explainers, documentaries or data stories, not "AI-generated content at scale."
- Reels-style output (short, templated, high volume) is attacked from three sides: the inauthentic-content policy, the October 2026 originality ranking in Shorts, and the 2027 Shorts floor of 10M views per 90 days. This is the weakest path for the "money" criterion and for "respect."
- The slop economy was concentrated in kids, religious quiz, fan-fiction and anime, and emotional-bait formats, and largely in non-English markets (South Korea, Pakistan, India, Spain). Those niches carry the highest enforcement risk.

### Gaps
- No primary-source quote from Rene Ritchie or YouTube giving a quantitative threshold for "mass-produced."
- No verified named list of AI-heavy channels confirmed fully monetized in 2026. Vendor blogs claim "thousands" without verifiable names.
- No official YouTube statement confirming the specific January 2026 removals beyond the spokesperson remark about 11 channels.

## 3. Shorts monetization: revenue model, RPMs, view counting, 3-minute Shorts, and the Shorts-to-long-form funnel

### Takeaway
For YouTube, Shorts now earn as much as or more than long-form per watch hour in the US: parity in May 2025, "more" by October 2025. For creators, per-view RPM is still roughly 3-14% of long-form in most niches, with typical Shorts RPMs around $0.03 to $0.20. From February 2027, Shorts revenue also requires 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days. Shorts work as a reach and fame engine, not a revenue engine. Evidence that Shorts feed long-form is mixed and mostly vendor-sourced.

### Cited Findings
- **Revenue model:** Shorts ad revenue is pooled and creators receive 45% of the revenue allocated to them, versus 55% for long-form — [CreatiCalc](https://creaticalc.com/blog/how-much-does-youtube-shorts-pay); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-shorts-rpm/)
- **Platform-side parity:** in May 2025, Mohan said Shorts revenue per watch hour was on par with core YouTube in multiple countries including the US — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/youtube-shorts-revenue-per-watch-hour-engagement-neal-mohan/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-shorts-revenue-per-watch-hour-matches-traditional-video-in-us/)
- On the Q3 2025 call (29 October 2025), Sundar Pichai said: "In the US, Shorts now earn more revenue per watch hour than traditional in-stream on YouTube." Q3 2025 YouTube ad revenue was about $10.26B, up 15% year on year — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/youtube-ad-revenues-reached-10-3b-in-q3-2025-up-15-yoy-as-shorts-now-earn-more-revenue-per-watch-hour-than-long-form-content-in-the-us/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/10/29/shorts-now-make-more-money-for-youtube-than-long-form/)
- **Creator-side RPM (best dataset):** AIR Media-Tech's analysis of 274 channels using YouTube Analytics API data found Shorts RPM is 3-14% of long-form RPM in almost every niche. Music is the exception, where Content ID narrows the gap to 41-60%. Niche-level Shorts RPMs ranged from about $0.02 to $1.48 — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-shorts-rpm-vs-long-form-how-much-do-shorts-earn-in-2026)
- Six long-form-focused creators told Digiday their Shorts RPMs were consistently below $0.20, against $3-6 on regular videos. This is cited second-hand — [Brightbean citing Digiday](https://brightbean.xyz/blog/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-revenue/)
- Vendor ranges: Shorts about $0.03-0.07 RPM versus long-form $5-30. Shorts payouts of about $30-200 per million views versus $2,000-10,000+ for long-form. These are community or vendor figures — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-rpm); [CreatiCalc](https://creaticalc.com/blog/how-much-does-youtube-shorts-pay)
- Possible cannibalization: in a small, self-selected sample, entertainment channels earned $4.58 long-form RPM in months without Shorts and $1.84 in months with Shorts — [MilX](https://milx.app/en/cases/how-much-do-youtube-shorts-pay-to-successful-creators)
- **View counting change (31 March 2025):** a Shorts "view" now counts whenever a Short starts or replays, with no minimum watch time. The old metric was renamed "engaged views" and kept in Advanced Mode — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/); [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/03/27/how-does-youtube-shorts-count-views-2025/); [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/how-are-views-counted-shorts-tiktok-reels/)
- Sources conflict on whether monetization and YPP eligibility use engaged views. One guide says eligibility and revenue share use engaged views or "qualified" views; another says the change does not affect eligibility or earnings — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/); [Async guide](https://async.com/blog/how-long-can-youtube-shorts-be/). The 2027 YPP terms use the phrase "qualified Shorts views" — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)
- **3-minute Shorts (older, October 2024):** square or vertical uploads up to 3 minutes, uploaded on or after 15 October 2024, are classed as Shorts and monetized under the Shorts revenue-sharing model. Any Short over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim is blocked globally and cannot be monetized — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)
- **Shorts scale:** Mohan said at Cannes Lions in June 2025 that Shorts average over 200B daily views. This is reported via secondary sources — [Axis Intelligence](https://axis-intelligence.com/youtube-shorts-statistics/); [Outlierkit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ceo-2026-strategic-letter/)
- **Funnel evidence (vendor):** one study found long-form converted 22.7 subscribers per 10,000 views versus 16.9 for Shorts, with the pattern reversing for channels above 1M subscribers. A vendor estimates 0.5-2% Shorts-viewer-to-subscriber conversion. Treat both as vendor figures; attribution within the results was unclear — [Upgrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-shorts-vs-longform-roi-2026/); [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/creators-spotlight/still-doubting-shorts-in-2026-were-not-heres-why)
- **2027 floor:** Shorts revenue sharing will require 10M qualified Shorts views per rolling 90 days, about 3.3M per month, from 1 February 2027 — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)

### Inferences
- At about $0.05-0.20 RPM, 10M Shorts views per 90 days earns roughly $500-2,000 per quarter (my arithmetic from the cited ranges). Shorts income for a sub-10M channel will be zero from February 2027. Shorts should be budgeted as discovery and brand reach (fame), not revenue.
- The view-counting change inflated Shorts view totals. Comparisons with pre-April 2025 benchmarks should use engaged views.
- The previous AI faceless-reels generator is best repurposed as a clip and teaser engine pointing at original long-form, not as the core product.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube disclosure of average Shorts RPM by country or niche.
- No rigorous independent study of whether Shorts audiences convert to long-form watch time for faceless channels specifically.
- The exact definition of "qualified Shorts views" in the 2027 terms was not confirmed from the primary text.

## 4. Format and viewing shifts: TV share, podcasts, long-form, CEO priorities, and YouTube's own AI tools

### Takeaway
The centre of gravity is the living room. YouTube is the largest US TV distributor in Nielsen's Gauge, at a record 14.2% of all US TV viewing in July 2026, and the most-used US podcast platform. Both CEO letters (2025: "the new television"; 2026: creators as "the new stars and studios" making "must-see TV") point toward produced, lean-back long-form. YouTube's own AI tools (Veo 3 Fast in Shorts, Gemini editing, Ask Studio, multi-language audio and auto-dubbing, likeness Shorts, Remix) lower production cost for everyone. That reduces the advantage of an AI production pipeline and increases the premium on originality and authority.

- Multi-language audio is especially favourable to faceless and narrated formats, where there is no lip-sync problem. It extends a single English production into global audiences (fame) at near-zero marginal cost.
- The Remix and likeness tools mean any successful original visual style can be cloned inside YouTube's own app. Brand and recognizable IP matter more than a visual format.

## 5. Advertiser-friendliness: limited-ads topics (true crime, war, health, finance) and Made-for-Kids

### Takeaway
Advertiser-friendly rules loosened in 2025-2026. Strong profanity in the first 7 seconds no longer demonetizes (July 2025). Non-graphic, dramatized controversial issues became fully monetizable (January 2026). Graphic violence, sensational true crime, tragedy and war footage, and unverified medical claims still tend to get limited ads; reports of an April 2026 tightening are unconfirmed. The bigger 2026 change for health, finance and legal topics is that AI personas giving advice are now "inauthentic" and non-monetizable. Made-for-Kids remains low-RPM with no personalized ads. AI age estimation since August 2025 removes personalized ads for teen-classified viewers. Advocacy pressure is building against AI kids' content.

### Cited Findings
- **Profanity (July 2025):** creators can earn full ad revenue on videos using strong profanity in the first seven seconds, reversing the stricter 2022 approach. Heavy profanity throughout, or strong profanity in titles or thumbnails, still limits ads. Slurs and extreme profanity remain barred — [YouTube Help, ad guideline updates](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9725604?hl=en); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-removes-strong-profanity-restrictions-from-first-7-seconds/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-eases-monetization-restrictions-content-profanity/756398/)
- **Controversial issues (January 2026):** content focused on controversial issues (abortion, self-harm, suicide, abuse) can now earn full ad revenue when non-graphic and dramatized. Previously, dramatized depictions were limited regardless of detail. Content centered on child abuse or eating disorders remains ineligible for full monetization — [MediaPost, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/411997/youtube-opens-ad-rev-monetization-for-dramatized-c.html); [YouTube Help, ad guideline updates](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9725604?hl=en)
- The September 2023 precedent (older): non-graphic discussion of abortion, sexual abuse and similar topics became fully monetizable — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/26/youtube-relaxes-advertiser-friendly-guidelines-around-controversial-topics-like-abortion-abuse-and-eating-disorders)
- **Sensitive events, war and tragedy:** the Help Center has a sensitive-events category. Creator guides say war, disaster and terrorism coverage often gets limited ads even when it is neutral journalism — [YouTube Help, advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en); [YT Money Calculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/youtube-demonetization-words-to-avoid/) (blog)
- **True crime:** graphic violence in true crime, even with documentary framing, often triggers limited or no ads — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/true-crime-videos-guide) (blog). The 2025 termination of the fabricated AI channel "True Crime Case Files" shows the deception risk in this niche — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/02/14/youtube-true-crime-case-files-ai-crime-channel-terminated/)
- **Claimed April 2026 enforcement wave (unconfirmed):** one blog says mental-health crisis content, extreme weight loss, unverified medical claims and sensationalized true crime moved toward demonetization, and that Shorts are now assessed per video. I could not confirm this against a YouTube announcement — [AuditSocials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/youtube-shorts-monetization-advertiser-friendly-guidelines-april-2026-new-enforcement-wave)
- **Health, finance and legal advice via AI personas:** listed as non-monetizable inauthentic content from July 2026 — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-youtube-ai-video-monetization-rules/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2226445/youtube-explains-ai-slop-policy-why-some-creators-wont-get-paid/)
- **AI labels on sensitive topics:** health, news, elections and finance content may get a more prominent on-player "altered or synthetic" label — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/ai-disclosure-rules/)
- **Made-for-Kids and teens:** AI age estimation rolled out to US viewers in August 2025. It infers under-18 viewers regardless of the stated birthday and turns off personalized ads for them, with verification by ID, credit card or selfie — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3031888); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/youtube-ai-powered-technology-teen-users/)
- Made-for-Kids is a video label and is separate from viewer age estimation. A creator blog lists 22 April 2026 as the compliance date for the amended COPPA Rule; this was not confirmed against the FTC — [Sprizzy](https://sprizzy.com/blog/youtube-coppa-changes-as-of-january-2020-explained/)
- "Protecting kids and teens" is one of the four 2026 CEO priorities — [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/social-media/articles/youtube-ceo-neal-mohan-big-140000253.html)
- An advocacy coalition (Fairplay, March 2026) is asking YouTube to exclude AI-generated content from YouTube Kids — [Fairplay for Kids](https://fairplayforkids.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/YouTube-Letter-AI-Slop.pdf)

### Inferences
- AI-generated kids' content is the highest-risk niche on several fronts: low Made-for-Kids monetization with no personalized ads, a 2026 CEO priority on kids' safety, advocacy pressure for AI exclusion, and kids-adjacent formats prominent in the slop takedowns.
- True crime, war and history-of-atrocity niches can build large audiences (fame), but face recurring limited ads and deception scrutiny. Accuracy and non-graphic treatment are required to monetize.
- Finance, health and legal content remains high-CPM, but on YouTube a credible human presenter or real expert sourcing is now effectively a requirement. Anonymous AI-persona formats are explicitly excluded.
- The 2025-2026 loosening of profanity and dramatized-controversy rules modestly favours scripted, narrative and commentary formats.

### Gaps
- No primary YouTube text confirming any 2025-2026 change specific to true crime or health misinformation monetization.
- No confirmed 2026 YouTube announcement on Made-for-Kids monetization changes or AI kids' content rules.
- No public CPM data by sensitive topic from YouTube.

## 6. Search and recommendation changes from AI answers (Google AI Overviews, YouTube "Ask", AI summaries)

### Takeaway
Two forces pull in opposite directions. YouTube is a top source cited by Google AI Overviews, especially for how-to and instructional queries, with vendor studies reporting citation growth of 25% to 400%+ in 2025. Meanwhile, YouTube's own Gemini "Ask" button (from late 2025) and "Ask YouTube" conversational search (a US Premium test from May 2026) can answer viewers' questions without a full watch. That threatens watch time for simple how-to content. I found no hard data on how-to click or watch-time losses.

### Cited Findings
- Search Engine Land (2025): YouTube citations in Google AI Overviews rose 25.21%. Instructional content rose 35.6%, with how-to queries leading, and visual demonstrations rose 32.5% — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/youtube-citations-google-ai-overviews-surge-2025-451852)
- NP Digital and Neil Patel: a 414% rise in YouTube citations overall and 651% for how-to queries. The methodology is inconsistent across write-ups, and the figures may not be independent — [Neil Patel](https://neilpatel.com/marketing-stats/youtube-ai-overviews/); [Neil Patel](https://neilpatel.com/marketing-stats/youtube-citations-ai-overviews/)
- Otterly (2026): over half of the social-media citations in Google AI Overviews and AI Mode come from YouTube, and 31% of cited videos contained timestamp signals — [Otterly](https://otterly.ai/blog/youtube-ai-citation-study-2026/)
- The Stacc (1,000 queries): YouTube accounts for about 23.3% of AI Overview citations — [The Stacc](https://thestacc.com/blog/ai-overviews-citation-sources/)
- Alian Software (vendor) claims its analysis of 100M+ AI citations found view counts and subscriber numbers had no meaningful link to citation frequency, and that long-form, reference-style videos made up 94% of citations — [Alian Software](https://aliansoftware.com/en/blog/geo-for-youtube-ai-citation-signal)
- **In-video Gemini "Ask" button:** rolled out from about October/November 2025 on mobile and desktop, staged by region. It lets viewers ask questions about a video and get summaries — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/youtube-release-gemini-powered-ask-feature-pc-smartphone-how-it-works-125111200565_1.html); [Geekflare](https://geekflare.com/news/youtube-adds-gemini-ai-powered-ask-button/)
- **"Ask YouTube" (Google I/O, 19 May 2026):** conversational search that aggregates Shorts and long-form results with short AI text summaries of whether each video fits the query. It is available to US Premium members via YouTube Labs as a limited test — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/05/19/youtube-search-adds-ask-youtube-gemini-omni-for-shorts-remix/); [Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2026/05/20/youtube-ask-youtube-gemini-omni-ai-google-io/)
- A commentator warned the Ask feature could "stop people from watching the content", by analogy with Google's AI summaries, and said the monetization effect for creators is unclear — [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/gemini/articles/youtubes-ask-button-just-solved-133016252.html)
- Made On 2026 added custom feeds built from conversational prompts (US, fall 2026), a further shift from keyword search to AI-mediated discovery — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-presents-new-ai-and-engagement-features-at-made-on-2026/831216/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/youtube-releases-new-ai-features-for-creators-within-its-studio-app/)
- Under the July 2026 rules, tutorials that copy existing platform material are classed as templated inauthentic content — [The AI Insider](https://theaiinsider.tech/2026/07/21/youtube-clarifies-monetization-policies-to-further-curb-ai-generated-slop-content/)

### Inferences
- Simple, answerable how-to content (single-fact tutorials) faces a double squeeze. AI answers summarize it, and copy-style tutorials are now policy-risky. Structured, reference-grade long-form with chapters and timestamps is what AI Overviews cite, which may bring authority and discovery (respect) even if click-through declines.
- Content whose value is the experience (narrative, entertainment, personality, story, visual spectacle) is less exposed to answer engines than content whose value is information retrieval.
- Discovery is moving toward AI-mediated surfaces (Ask YouTube, prompt-built feeds, AI Overviews). Clear topical authority and well-structured metadata likely matter more than keyword-stuffed titles (inference; no direct data).

### Gaps
- No independent data quantifying lost YouTube clicks or watch time on how-to videos due to Google AI Overviews or YouTube's Ask features.
- No YouTube statement on whether Ask or summary interactions count toward watch time or affect creator revenue.
- Citation studies are mostly from SEO vendors with inconsistent methods. Treat them as directional.
