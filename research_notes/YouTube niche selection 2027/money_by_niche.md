# YouTube Monetization Economics by Niche and Sub-Niche (2025-2026)

Research date: October 2026. Scope: ad RPM/CPM, Shorts RPM, sponsorship rates, affiliate and digital-product potential by niche, plus geography, language, Q4 seasonality and faceless/AI channel economics.

**Read this first: how reliable these numbers are.**
- WebFetch could not resolve any domain in this session, so no source page was opened in full. Every figure below comes from search-engine result summaries of the cited pages. Treat each number as "reported by that page per search snippet," not verified on the page. The web-search budget also ran out before a few planned follow-ups (named creator RPM disclosures, faceless sponsorship penalty, India/LatAm CPM trends). Those are listed under Gaps.
- Only one dataset found is both measured and recent: AIR Media-Tech's 2025-2026 studies (300 channels for long-form RPM, 274 for Shorts, May 2025 to May 2026, from the YouTube Analytics API). It is global, not US-heavy. AIR is an MCN whose creator base is largely international (background knowledge; not verified this session). So its medians sit well below US-centric blog figures.
- Most per-niche "US RPM" ranges come from SEO/calculator blogs (Fluxnote, YouTubeRPMCalculator, Lenostube, Vloggingpro, CreatiCalc, Hubfluence, OutlierKit). They rarely state a method, and they differ by 2-5x for the same niche. Treat them as rough US-audience ranges, often on the optimistic side.
- Sponsorship figures come mostly from agency and vendor rate guides (Influencer Marketing Hub, OutlierKit, Dangaro, InfluencerFee, Hubfluence). No ThoughtLeaders, Spotter, Passionfroot, Agentio or Lumanu dataset surfaced in search.

## 1. What do 2025-2026 creator-reported RPM datasets show by niche?

### Takeaway
The only rigorous 2025-2026 dataset (AIR, 300 mostly non-US channels) puts the all-niche median long-form RPM at about $2.30. Education & Science leads at a $10.22 median, Kids & Teens trails at $0.33, and Business & Finance comes in at only $2.01, though that last figure rests on fewer than 10 channels. US-centric blog benchmarks still rank finance, legal/insurance and real estate highest ($10-$50 RPM claims), then education, history, true crime and automotive ($5-$15), with gaming, music, kids and news at the bottom ($0.30-$5). Spread *within* a niche (Education: $2.31-$19.50) is often larger than the gap between niches.

### Inferences
- **Reconciling AIR and blog figures:** AIR's US advertiser CPM ($14.67) times the 55% creator share times a typical 50-70% monetized-playback rate implies an all-niche US RPM of roughly $4-$6. A 1.5-3x premium for high-value niches (finance, legal, education, B2B tech) gives about $8-$18 RPM for a strongly US-heavy channel in those niches. That fits the lower-middle of blog ranges. Blog upper bounds ($35-$55) likely reflect top-decile channels or CPM-like numbers.
- AIR's low Business & Finance median ($2.01, under 10 channels) almost certainly reflects non-US audiences. Do not read it as "finance pays poorly." Read it as "finance pays well only with Tier-1 viewers."
- Education & Science leading AIR's global table is notable. It suggests education draws Tier-1 viewers and brand-safe advertisers even in an international sample, which favors faceless explainer, history and science formats.
- Niche-level seasonality: no niche-specific Q4 data was found. Retail- and gift-driven niches (tech reviews, gaming, luxury, toys/kids, home/DIY) plausibly see the largest Q4 lift. Finance and career niches may see a January demand bump (new-year budgeting, job search). This is speculation and should be labeled as such.
- Plan on a Q4 AdSense uplift of roughly +10-40%. The AIR creator median shows a small bump, while blog and ad-platform data show a large one. The true figure likely depends on whether a channel's ad inventory is US retail-heavy.

### Gaps
- No measured 2025-2026 US-only RPM dataset by niche was found. All US niche ranges are blog estimates.
- No measured RPM was found for: luxury/watches, ASMR specifically, language learning, career/upskilling, home improvement/DIY, travel (AIR "Lifestyle" is the only proxy), food/cooking (AIR median not visible), sports alone (AIR merges Health & Sport), space (science proxy only), software tutorials, side hustles.
- AIR's News & Politics and Food & Cooking medians, and the full P25-P75 bands for every niche, were not visible in snippets (the page could not be fetched).
- No niche-specific Q4 or January seasonality data was found.
- No 2025-2026 creator-disclosed RPMs for named channels (e.g., Ali Abdaal, Graham Stephan) surfaced. Graham Stephan's Wikipedia entry cites only **2020-2021** income ($5.1M in 2020, about half from YouTube ads) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Stephan).

## 2. Which niches have the highest total revenue per view once sponsorship and affiliate are included?

### Takeaway
Once sponsorships and affiliate income are added, finance (especially credit cards, investing and tax), B2B SaaS/software tutorials, legal/insurance and AI/tech tools pull far ahead. Their sponsor CPMs ($30-$80+) and per-conversion affiliate payouts ($50-$500) dwarf AdSense. Education, science and history do well on AdSense but have thinner affiliate economics. Gaming, music/relaxation, kids and news remain low-yield per view, though they offer volume. Digital products (courses) are largest in health/fitness, business/finance and personal development, going by Kajabi's lifetime revenue totals.

How the "total $ per 1K views" column is built [I]: AdSense RPM, plus one integration per video at the niche's sponsor CPM (assumes the sponsor pays on the video's views, and that sponsors fill only 30-70% of videos for small and mid channels), plus affiliate. ## 3. Typical sponsorship rates in 2025-2026 and which categories have the most sponsor demand

### Takeaway
Integration pricing clusters around $10-$50 per 1,000 views, with a cited median of about $15 CPM for a 60-second read. Finance and B2B SaaS reach $40-$80+ and gaming sits at $10-$25. Flat fees by subscriber tier run from about $20-$500 (nano) and $200-$1K+ (micro) to $1K-$10K+ (100K-500K subscribers) and $20K-$50K+ (1M+). Demand is led by software/SaaS/AI tools (about 19% of sponsoring brands in one sample) and VPN/privacy (about 11%), followed by consumer tech and finance (investing/crypto and financial services about 7% each). ## 4. Shorts monetization in practice: RPM by niche, and can a Shorts-first channel make meaningful money?

### Takeaway
Shorts RPM is roughly $0.02-$0.33 in most niches, about 3-14% of long-form, per AIR's 274-channel study. A US-heavy audience tops out around $0.33. Music with owned rights is the exception at $0.97-$1.48. One million Shorts views is worth about $10-$200. A rule change from February 1, 2027 makes Shorts-first strategies much weaker for small channels: a channel needs 10M qualified Shorts views in the prior 90 days to earn from the Shorts Creator Pool at all, and new YPP applicants via Shorts need 20M views in 90 days. Shorts are a discovery funnel, not a revenue engine, except at very large scale.

### Cited Findings
- AIR (274 channels, YouTube Analytics API, full-year medians): Shorts RPM ranges **$0.02-$1.48** across 13 niches, at roughly **3-14% of long-form RPM**. Most niches cluster at $0.02-$0.20. Music is the only niche where the gap closes: small music channels $1.48, medium $0.97. — [AIR Shorts RPM by niche](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/what-is-youtube-shorts-rpm-in-your-niche-in-2026)
- One secondary source says the music premium applies mainly when the creator owns the rights, since the revenue is largely Content ID on owned music. — [search summary citing a write-up on AIR data; see Overseeros](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-rpm-by-niche) (attribution uncertain)
- **Shorts RPM by audience country (AIR partner data):** US $0.328, Switzerland $0.205, Australia $0.193, South Korea $0.185, Japan $0.144, New Zealand $0.113, India $0.008. Large channels with non-US audiences earn $0.03-$0.10. — [AIR: Shorts RPM in 2026](https://air.io/en/monetization/what-rpm-can-you-expect-from-shorts-in-2026); [MilX via search](https://milx.app/en/cases/how-much-do-youtube-shorts-pay-to-successful-creators)
- Blog Shorts RPM by niche: Finance $0.15-$0.45 and Entertainment/Comedy $0.01-$0.05 — [Virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/most-profitable-ai-youtube-shorts-niches-2026-rpm-data). Finance $0.06-$0.10 and Entertainment $0.03-$0.05 (US) — [YTCalculators](https://ytcalculators.com/blog/youtube-rpm-by-niche/). Global Shorts average $0.03-$0.08 — [pooled via search](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/average-youtube-rpm/). One source notes Shorts revenue is pooled by country first, so per-niche Shorts CPM is partly an estimate — [pooled via search](https://www.learningrevolution.net/youtube-earnings-calculator/).
- Per 1M Shorts views: $50-$200, $10-$100, or about $10-$60, depending on source. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/how-much-do-youtubers-make/); [Learning Revolution](https://www.learningrevolution.net/how-much-money-does-youtube-pay-for-1-million-views/); [Stan Store](https://stan.store/blog/how-much-does-youtube-pay/)
- **Case data:** 43 Shorts produced 20.8M views but only 9.5M engaged views (45.7%), earning **$259.56**, about $0.027 per 1K engaged views. Total channel revenue (mixed long and short) rose from $6,380 to $9,474 (+48%). — [MilX case study](https://milx.app/en/cases/how-much-will-you-get-for-10-millions-views-on-youtube-shorts). Reddit-reported (unverified): 130M+ Shorts views earned about $12,000 in a year; another creator earned $4.92 on 100K+ views. — [via MilX/IMH search summaries](https://milx.app/en/cases/how-much-do-youtube-shorts-pay-to-successful-creators)
- In 2025 YouTube changed Shorts view counting so a view registers on every start or replay. Pre-2025 per-view earnings claims are not directly comparable. — [vidIQ Shorts monetization](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-monetization/); [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/youtube-shorts-monetization)
- **YPP changes effective Feb 1, 2027 (confirmed on YouTube Help and in multiple reports):**
  - Existing partners must have **10M qualified Shorts views in the prior 90 days** to earn from the Shorts Creator Pool (ad and Premium revenue) for a given month. There is no partial payout: 9.9M views earns nothing from the pool that month. Long-form keeps earning. — [YouTube Help: Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-cuts-shorts-pay-for-channels-under-10m-views-from-february-2027/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-updates-partner-program-terms-shorts-payout-rules/585331/)
  - New applicants need **8,000 public watch hours in 365 days (up from 4,000) or 20M Shorts views in 90 days (up from 10M)**, plus 1,000 subscribers. The 500-subscriber fan-funding tier is unchanged. — [vidIQ blog](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/); [vidIQ on X](https://x.com/vidIQ/status/2086836464462008712); [TechTimes, Aug 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323954/20260811/youtube-partner-program-doubles-entry-bar-adds-monthly-shorts-earnings-gate.htm)
  - Sources disagree on details for existing partners, including whether updated terms must be accepted by January 31, 2027 and changes to the Premium revenue split. — [vidIQ blog](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/); [CreatiCalc](https://creaticalc.com/blog/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027)

### Inferences
- **Shorts-first economics after Feb 2027:** a channel at exactly 10M Shorts views per 90 days (about 3.3M a month) with a US-heavy audience earns roughly $0.10-$0.33 per 1K, or about $330-$1,100 a month from the pool. With a global audience it earns $100-$330 a month. Below the floor it earns $0. A Shorts-only channel is not a meaningful money model for a new channel. Shorts are useful mainly to funnel viewers to long-form, affiliate links and products.
- The new 8,000-watch-hour entry bar favors long-form formats with long average view duration (documentaries, history, explainers, 20-60 minute sleep/ambient content). That is a structural advantage for faceless documentary-style production.
- Music/sleep Shorts with owned audio (original AI-assisted or commissioned music) are the one Shorts category with near-long-form RPM. Rights ownership is essential.

### Gaps
- No per-niche Shorts RPM for education, finance or tech was visible in AIR snippets beyond the overall range.
- No verified 2025-2026 Shorts-only channel income disclosure was found (only anecdotes).

## 6. How do RPMs differ for non-English markets, and which large-language markets are under-served with rising CPMs?
